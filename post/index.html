Typography System & Editorial Guidelines: Clinical Content Standards for My Radiology Guide

A unified visual and semantic specification for body typography, heading hierarchies, diagnostic data structures, evidence citations, clinical callouts, and interactive widgets powering every protocol and atlas across our platform.

Published September 2026 · Clinical Design Board

Medical knowledge delivery demands rigorous visual hierarchy, high typographic contrast, and uncompromising semantic clarity. This living editorial specification outlines how each branch of our design system is deployed across all radiology articles, scanning protocols, and interactive tools on My Radiology Guide.

01 · Text & Inline Semantics

The lead paragraph establishes the clinical scope of every article. It is set at 16.5px with a line-height of 1.75 and a medium font weight to invite the eye into the technical literature.

Standard paragraphs form the analytical backbone of our educational literature. Set at 16px with an optimum 65–75 character line length, this size maximizes readability under high-stress diagnostic conditions. Every inline typographic style carries specific clinical purpose:

  • Bold Emphasis (<strong>): Reserved exclusively for pathognomonic radiological signs, pivotal anatomical structures, and definitive diagnoses—such as the hyperdense MCA sign, the comb sign in Crohn disease, or the air crescent sign in aspergilloma.
  • Italic Nuance (<em>): Employed for formal anatomical Latin nomenclature (e.g., falx cerebri, fissura longitudinalis cerebri), technical nuances, or gentle contextual emphasis.
  • Text Highlight (<mark>): Highlights actionable diagnostic cutoff points and clinical score triggers, such as ASPECTS < 6 for thrombectomy eligibility, or eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m² for contrast screening thresholds.
  • Small Annotations (<small>): Utilized for scanner calibration footnotes, detector collimation fine-print, and manufacturer-specific algorithm caveats (e.g., Siemens SAFIRE Level 3 vs. GE ASiR-V 50% iterative reconstruction kernels).
  • Subscript (<sub>): Formats chemical compounds and contrast formulations, such as CO2 insufflation in colonography, H2O baseline attenuation, and I- ion release in non-ionic formulations.
  • Superscript (<sup>): Denotes volumetric dosimetry units (e.g., 140 cm3, 2.5 mm2), radiopharmaceutical isotopes (such as 99mTc-sestamibi and 18F-FDG), and reference citations[1].

§ 03 · Lists & Taxonomy

03 · Lists & Taxonomy Systems

Different clinical scenarios dictate specialized list architectures. We deploy standard bulleted lists, numbered algorithms, multi-tier nested taxonomies, interactive procedural checklists, and clinical definition lists:

A. Unordered Bullet List (Diagnostic Differentials)

  • Non-contrast hyperdense vessel sign: Sensitive for acute intraluminal thrombus within the first 90 minutes.
  • Loss of insular ribbon: Earliest cortical sign due to vulnerability of insular collateral watersheds.
  • Obscuration of lentiform nucleus: Early intracellular cytotoxic edema of putamen and globus pallidus.

B. Ordered Numbered List (Sequential Stroke Triage Workflow)

  1. Perform non-contrast head CT (NCCT) to rule out intracranial hemorrhage and establish baseline ASPECTS.
  2. Execute Phase 1 CTA (aortic arch to vertex) with bolus tracking trigger at descending aorta (100 HU threshold).
  3. Execute Phase 2 (skull base to vertex) at 8-second delay to assess peak venous collateral retrograde filling.
  4. Execute Phase 3 (late venous) at subsequent 8-second delay to assess clearance and residual microvascular washout.

C. Nested Hierarchical List

  • Anterior Circulation Territorials:
    • Internal Carotid Artery (ICA):
      • Cervical & petrous segments (C1–C2)
      • Cavernous & clinoid segments (C3–C5)
      • Terminus bifurcation (C6–C7)
    • Middle Cerebral Artery (MCA):
      • M1 horizontal sphenoidal segment
      • M2 insular branches
      • M3 opercular & M4 cortical convexity

E. Clinical Definition List (Anatomical & Hemodynamic Lexicon)

Ischemic Core
Non-viable, irreversibly infracted brain tissue characterized by severe cerebral blood flow reduction (< 30% of normal contralateral tissue) and ADC reduction < 620 × 10-6 mm2/s.
Penumbra
Hypoperfused, structurally intact tissue surrounding the core that remains potentially salvageable if arterial recanalization occurs within the therapeutic time window.
Mismatch Ratio
The quantitative quotient of critically hypoperfused tissue volume (Tmax > 6.0 seconds) divided by ischemic core volume (rCBF < 30%); a ratio ≥ 1.8 indicates favorable mechanical thrombectomy candidacy.

Diagnostic Physics Formula Cards

EQ. 01 · Inverse Square Law for Radiation Protection
$$\frac{I_1}{I_2} = \frac{d_2^2}{d_1^2} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad I_2 = I_1 \times \left(\frac{d_1}{d_2}\right)^2$$

Doubling physical distance from a fluoroscopy scatter source reduces the operator exposure rate to exactly 25% (one-fourth) of initial intensity.

I₁, I₂ Radiation exposure rate or intensity (μGy/s or mR/h)
d₁, d₂ Distance from the radiation scatter or tube focal point (meters)
EQ. 02 · Volumetric CT Dose Index (CTDIvol)
$$\text{CTDI}_{\text{vol}} = \frac{\text{CTDI}_w}{\text{Pitch}} = \frac{\frac{1}{3}\,\text{CTDI}_{\text{center}} + \frac{2}{3}\,\text{CTDI}_{\text{periphery}}}{\text{Pitch}}$$

Calculates the average absorbed dose across a standardized 16 cm (head) acrylic phantom over the full irradiated helical volume.

CTDI_w Weighted CT Dose Index: ⅓ central + ⅔ peripheral dose
Pitch Table feed per rotation divided by total collimation width
EQ. 03 · Dose-Length Product (DLP) & Effective Dose (E)
$$\text{DLP} = \text{CTDI}_{\text{vol}} \times L \quad [\text{mGy}\cdot\text{cm}], \qquad E = k \times \text{DLP} \quad [\text{mSv}]$$

Quantifies cumulative total radiation burden across the entire scan range and normalizes biological risk using tissue weighting factors.

L Total anatomical scan length along patient z-axis (cm)
k Region conversion factor (k = 0.0021 mSv·mGy⁻¹·cm⁻¹ for Head & Neck)
EQ. 04 · Hounsfield Unit (HU) Physical Attenuation Equation
$$\text{HU} = 1000 \times \left( \frac{\mu_{\text{tissue}} - \mu_{\text{water}}}{\mu_{\text{water}}} \right)$$

Defines linear X-ray attenuation coefficients relative to pure water (0 HU) and ambient room air (-1000 HU) across monochromatic and polychromatic spectra.

μ_tissue Linear attenuation coefficient of sampled anatomical tissue (cm⁻¹)
μ_water Linear attenuation coefficient of distilled water at reference kVp

Scientific & Radiological Notation

Dosimetry values and isotope activities are formatted using monospace scientific notation tokens: 2.4 × 10³ mGy·cm (Dose-Length Product), 3.7 × 10¹⁰ Bq (1 Curie activity), and 1.2 × 10⁻⁴ mSv/mAs.

§ 11 · Clinical Callouts

11 · Clinical Callouts & Alert Modalities

We provide six standardized callout containers engineered with distinct chromatic signals to communicate urgency, safety, and board exam relevance:

Clinical Note

NCCT attenuation values can be falsely elevated in patients with severe polycythemia (hematocrit > 55%), occasionally mimicking the hyperdense artery sign in patent vessels.

Technique Tip

Elevate patient arms above the head for torso scans, but maintain slight adduction for neck/head scans to eliminate beam hardening and clavicular streak artifact across the carotid bifurcation.

Important Protocol Mandate

Immediately notify the neuro-interventional on-call fellow if an ICA terminus occlusion ("T-lesion") or proximal M1 thrombus is identified on Phase 1, even prior to completion of reconstructed thin-slice MIP series.

Patient Safety Warning: Contrast Extravasation

If contrast extravasation occurs at flow rates ≥ 4.5 mL/s, stop injection immediately, aspirate residual fluid if feasible, elevate limb above heart level, apply cold compress, and monitor for acute compartment syndrome.

Formal Diagnostic Criteria: ASPECTS

Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score: A 10-point quantitative topographical scoring system dividing the MCA territory into 10 regions; 1 point is subtracted for each region demonstrating early parenchymal hypoattenuation.

Core Exam High-Yield Pearl

Board examiners frequently test the differential between acute cytotoxic edema and vasogenic edema: cytotoxic edema involves both gray and white matter (loss of gray-white boundary), whereas vasogenic edema spares the cortex and respects the gray-white junction (finger-like projections in deep white matter).

A. Multi-Modality Parameter Tabs

Computed Tomography Angiography (mCTA)

Dual-source or 64+ slice CT system. Acquisition speed: 0.5 s rotation time. 70 mL Omnipaque 350 at 5.0 mL/s via right antecubital 18G vein with automated bolus trigger at descending thoracic aorta (100 HU threshold).

Magnetic Resonance Stroke Protocol (1.5T / 3.0T)

DWI (b=0, b=1000 s/mm2) with ADC mapping, axial FLAIR (to assess DWI-FLAIR mismatch < 4.5 hours), 3D Time-of-Flight (TOF) MRA without contrast, and axial T2* gradient-echo or SWI to exclude microbleeds.

Carotid & Vertebral Doppler Ultrasound

Linear array high-frequency probe (7–12 MHz). Measure peak systolic velocity (PSV) and end-diastolic velocity (EDV) in distal CCA, bulb, and proximal ICA. PSV > 230 cm/s indicates ≥ 70% internal carotid stenosis by NASCET criteria.

C. Diagnostic Troubleshooting Accordion

How do you differentiate pseudo-occlusion from genuine ICA terminus occlusion?
Severe cervical ICA bulb stenosis or tortuosity can cause "contrast stagnation" mimicking intracranial occlusion on early arterial Phase 1. Delayed venous Phase 2 and 3 scans will reveal late trickle filling into the petrous and cavernous siphon, confirming patency and sparing unnecessary intracranial intervention.
What is the management algorithm for severe intravenous contrast extravasation?
Immediately halt the injector and aspirate extravasated volume if the cannula is in place. Elevate the extremity above the level of the heart, apply cold packs (15–20 minutes at a time) to promote vasoconstriction and limit edema, measure limb circumference, check distal pulses, and consult plastic surgery if volume exceeds 100 mL or signs of compartment syndrome emerge.
How is the "DWI-FLAIR mismatch" used in wake-up stroke triage?
A lesion that is hyperintense on diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) but has not yet become hyperintense on FLAIR indicates that tissue ischemia began less than 4.5 hours ago. These "wake-up stroke" patients can safely receive IV thrombolysis (alteplase or tenecteplase) within standard window safety profiles.

D. Frequently Asked Questions (Clinical Implementation FAQ)

Q: Why is multiphase CTA superior to single-phase CTA for collateral assessment?
Single-phase CTA acquires only a snapshot at peak arterial timing. Patients with slow retrograde leptomeningeal flow often appear to have "no collaterals" on Phase 1, but reveal robust collateral reconstitution by Phase 2 or 3. Multiphase CTA prevents erroneously excluding salvageable stroke candidates.
Q: Are there radiation dose penalties when acquiring multiphase CTA?
Multiphase CTA uses low-dose acquisitions for Phase 2 and Phase 3 (restricted to the skull base to vertex, omitting the neck and chest). The combined radiation dose typically adds only 1.5–2.0 mSv over standard CTA, well below the radiation threshold of conventional CT perfusion acquisitions.
