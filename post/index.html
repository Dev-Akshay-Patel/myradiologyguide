Acute Ischemic Stroke: Multiphase CTA Collateral Atlas & ASPECTS Triage Protocol

Comprehensive emergency neuro-imaging algorithm for identifying salvageable penumbra, assessing core infarct ratios, and evaluating mechanical thrombectomy candidate triage within the extended window.

RG
Dr. Elena Vance, MD Attending Neuroradiologist
• Published March 8, 2026 • 7 min read • ARRT Certified Guide

In acute ischemic stroke triage, rapid and accurate neurovascular assessment dictates patient outcome. Modern management relies on multiphase computed tomography angiography (mCTA) and the Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (ASPECTS) to triage candidates for emergent endovascular thrombectomy (EVT).

1. Core Principles of Multiphase CTA

Single-phase CTA often underestimates collateral circulation in patients with slow or delayed retrograde flow. Multiphase CTA overcomes this hemodynamic limitation by acquiring three distinct phases during a single intravenous contrast bolus:

  • Phase 1 (Peak Arterial): Acquired from arch to vertex, visualizing proximal large vessel occlusions (LVO) including ICA terminus and MCA-M1 trunk.
  • Phase 2 (Peak Venous): Covers skull base to vertex, revealing leptomeningeal retrograde refill into ischemic territories.
  • Phase 3 (Late Venous): Captures delayed washout and residual microvascular pooling in penumbral tissue beds.
Clinical Radiology Pearl

A patient presenting with poor collaterals on single-phase CTA may demonstrate robust reconstitution on Phase 2 or 3 of multiphase CTA. Never disqualify an extended-window patient without reviewing delayed venous phases.

2. Standardized Collateral Grading System

Collaterals are categorized on a six-point scale (scores 0 to 5) or simplified into three actionable clinical tiers based on the extent and delay of vascular filling in the occluded MCA territory:

Grade Phase 1 (Arterial) Phase 2 (Peak Venous) Clinical Implications
Good (Tier 1) Normal or minimal delay in pial filling Symmetrical or greater vessel density High EVT benefit; low hemorrhage risk
Intermediate (Tier 2) Absent or visible filling lag (>2 phases) Partial asymmetry in vessel density Eligible if core infarct ratio is small
Poor (Tier 3) No collateral filling in ischemic bed Persistent vascular void High likelihood of malignant MCA syndrome

3. ASPECTS Metric Breakdown

The Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score divides the middle cerebral artery territory into 10 distinct anatomic regions on non-contrast CT (NCCT). Each area showing early ischemic changes (hypoattenuation or loss of grey-white distinction) deducts 1 point from an initial score of 10:

“An initial ASPECTS score ≥ 6 identifies patients with salvageable brain tissue who benefit most dramatically from mechanical recanalization within the 6 to 24-hour therapeutic window.”

Subcortical Structures (3 Points)

  1. C: Caudate nucleus head
  2. L: Lentiform nucleus (putamen & globus pallidus)
  3. IC: Internal capsule posterior limb

Cortical MCA Territories (7 Points)

  • M1: Anterior MCA cortex (frontal operculum)
  • M2: MCA cortex lateral to insular ribbon (anterior temporal)
  • M3: Posterior MCA cortex (posterior temporal)
  • I: Insular ribbon (early loss is highly sensitive)
  • M4, M5, M6: Rostral supraganglionic MCA territories at corona radiata level

4. Protocol Recommendations & Technique

For standard 64-slice or dual-source CT systems, administer 65–75 mL of non-ionic iodinated contrast (350–370 mg I/mL) at 4.5–5.0 mL/s, followed by a 40 mL saline flush. Trigger automatically via bolus tracking at the descending thoracic aorta with a 100 HU threshold. 

// Recommended Acquisition Timings
Phase 1: Bolus trigger + 4s delay (aortic arch to vertex)
Phase 2: Phase 1 end + 8s inter-scan delay (skull base to vertex)
Phase 3: Phase 2 end + 8s inter-scan delay (skull base to vertex)
Slice thickness: 0.625mm source; 1.25mm axial & coronal MIP reconstructions
